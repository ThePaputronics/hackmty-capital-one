The Last
Ten Seconds

A settled SPEI transfer is irrevocable by rule, and the loss falls on the person who was deceived into authorizing it. The only place left to intervene is before submission — and only the operator of the rail can build it.

The proposal

Instrument the moment before confirm, and give the ecosystem a shared account-level signal

Two layers. The first works inside any single institution today. The second is what makes it work nationally — and it is not something a third party can build in Mexico, which is why it belongs in this room.

Layer 1 — the product

Pre-submission intent guard

Scores the transfer in the seconds before it is sent and inserts reversible, explained friction. Every input is the payer's own observed behavior.

Layer 2 — the standard

CLABE-scoped flag registry

Account-level fraud signals. No names, no addresses, no amounts. Evidence counts, never verdicts.

The gap is structural, not operational

No participant is failing at its job. The rules themselves close off every recovery path after settlement.

SPEI identifies a payment by CLABE alone — the beneficiary name displayed to the sender verifies nothing. Once settled, a transfer is firm, irrevocable and enforceable against third parties. When the customer authenticated correctly and authorized the transfer themselves, neither institution is automatically liable, and the loss remains with the person who was deceived.

Authentication is not the failing control. In this fraud every authentication succeeds, because the customer really is the customer. The unanswered question is whether the authenticated customer is being manipulated — and nothing in the current stack asks it.

Complaint counts are complaints, not unique victims or confirmed losses. We hold to that distinction throughout; 1.515 million is a quarterly complaint volume, not a headcount.

Why no one else can fix the receiver side

This is the argument for why the proposal is addressed here rather than to a bank or a fintech.

There is no public registry of CLABEs of any kind. A CLABE can be validated and attributed to an institution entirely offline — the structure, the check-digit algorithm and the institution catalog are public. But the mapping from CLABE to holder, to history, or to any flag does not exist publicly, and secreto bancario (Art. 142 LIC) closes the private route.

So a third party can tell a payer "this account is new to you." It cannot tell them "this account has been reported by eleven other people this month." That second sentence requires an ecosystem-level facility, and the institution that operates the rail is the only credible home for it.

Brazil already built this

Banco Central do Brasil operates account-level fraud marking inside DICT, the Pix key directory. The mechanism is proven — and its costs are now visible too.

VERIFIED

Only your own customer. An institution may create a fraud marking only against a customer of its own, and the marking must be tied to a specific Pix transaction. That is the Sybil defense — the reporting party is a supervised entity, accountable for the claim.

VERIFIED

Markings decay on tiered windows of 90 days, 12 months and 60 months; beyond 60 months they become invisible to DICT users.

VERIFIED

Resolução BCB nº 506, published 26 September 2025 and in force from 30 September, introduced transactional fraud marking and obliges participants to hold an internal policy defining criteria, triggers and governance.

What is already going wrong there is the more useful half. Brazilian legal and press commentary describes people who unknowingly received scam funds having balances blocked and accounts marked. That is the mule-account holder — frequently an elderly relative whose ID was used — bearing the entire consequence while the recruiter, who never holds an account, stays invisible. Two published facts that have not, as far as we can find, been put side by side.

The cap was set at the top of normal behavior, so that it would not inconvenience the other 95%. The consequence is that a scammer pursuing a meaningful sum has exactly two options — and both leave a signature on the rail.

We are not proposing new infrastructure. We are proposing the layer that connects what is already operating.

CEP is the key that unlocks reporting without surveillance. Require a Banxico-sealed CEP as proof of standing to report a CLABE: the reporter must have actually transferred to that account. Attack cost rises from zero to real, traceable pesos, and the centre verifies standing cryptographically without ever seeing a name or mining the rail. Brazil's own-customer-only rule is the supervised-entity equivalent; both are worth carrying.

Governance is the design, not the disclaimer

Remove any one of these and the registry becomes a weapon for competitors and abusers.

01

Evidence counts, never verdicts. Show “3 independent reports in 30 days.” Never render a label that reads as an accusation.

02

Independent reporters, not report volume. Ten reports from one party is one signal. Reporter-reputation scoring is not a defense — it falls to simple collusion.

03

Standing is earned by a transaction, never asserted. No CEP, no report.

04

Flags decay on tiered visibility windows, as DICT does.

05

The right to know and to contest, built in from the first commit. LFPDPPP already grants rectification and opposition; a registry without a contest path is both unjust and unlawful.

06

Asymmetric consequence. A flag informs a payer's own decision. It never blocks an account and never travels as an instruction to an institution.

07

No free burn-check. Open querying lets criminals test whether mule accounts are still clean. Answer only within a pending transfer, rate-limited, never exposing the count.

On privacy, stated precisely: “CLABE only, no names” is pseudonymization, not anonymization. A CLABE is linkable to its holder by the issuing institution, so this remains personal data under LFPDPPP. We claim no exemption — only minimization, a stated consent basis, a retention limit and a contest process.

What could go wrong

Named up front, because a proposal that hides its failure modes is not ready to be evaluated.

01

There is no public ground truth for APP fraud in Mexico. CONDUSEF's categories capture transactions a customer says they did not authorize — the opposite of this fraud. Our models are trained on synthetic labels and we say so before we are asked.

02

False positives delay legitimate money. We report the challenge rate as prominently as the detection rate.

03

The innocent receiver. A mule-account holder is usually a victim too. Vulnerability must raise the “being exploited” hypothesis above “criminal” and route to outreach, never restriction. This is the failure Brazil is living through now.

04

A legitimate merchant looks like a mule on fan-in alone. Unsolved, this design flags most small businesses in Mexico. It has to be answered before anything ships.

05

Cold start. CONDUSEF complaints are filed against institutions, not accounts, so the registry cannot bootstrap from them. It seeds from behavioral detections contributed by participants.
