# Research 03 — Fraud, Transfers, Account Takeover & Digital Financial Crime in Mexico

Scope: CONDUSEF fraud statistics, dominant typologies (phishing/smishing, SIM swapping, vishing/falso ejecutivo, montadeudas), CoDi/DiMo/SPEI adoption and security, APP-fraud liability, regulatory levers (CNBV/Banxico/Ley Fintech), consumer evidence, and bank-side defenses. All findings below are labeled **VERIFIED** (URL cited, cross-checked against at least the search snippet/fetch content) or **UNVERIFIED/ANECDOTAL** (could not be confirmed against a primary source in the time available). Every peso and percentage figure is dated to the year it was reported.

## 1. Top pain points (ranked)

### #1 — Authorized push-payment (APP) / social-engineering fraud over SPEI, with the user bearing the loss
- **Population affected:** CONDUSEF/Buró de Entidades Financieras (BEF) logged **1.515 million fraud-related complaints** in Jan–Mar 2026 alone (up 31.5% YoY), out of 2.008 million total complaints (fraud = ~75% of all complaints against financial institutions). In full-year 2023, **4.9 million** fraud claims were filed. These are claims, not unique victims, but the order of magnitude (millions/year) is consistent across periods. **VERIFIED** — [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cartera/suben-quejas-por-fraudes-bancos-devuelven-24/), [El Informador](https://www.informador.mx/economia/fraude-reclamos-por-las-estafas-bancarias-suben-31.5-20260706-0024.html), [El Universal (2023 record)](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cartera/fraudes-a-usuarios-de-la-banca-rompen-record/)
- **Specific pain:** When a customer is socially engineered (vishing, fake bank officer, SMS phishing) into personally authorizing a SPEI transfer, Mexican rules treat the transfer as **firm, irrevocable and enforceable against third parties once it settles** in the beneficiary account, because SPEI payments are legally identified only by CLABE/account/card number — **the receiver's name is not a verified identifier**, even if it's shown on screen. Per CONDUSEF's own explanation of a documented SPEI scam, if the CLABE doesn't match the displayed name, **neither the sending nor receiving bank is liable**. Practically, liability falls on the user for authorized transfers. **VERIFIED (secondary synthesis of CONDUSEF explainer, not the raw regulatory text)** — [Infobae, Sep 2025](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2025/09/20/fraude-con-transferencias-electronicas-asi-opera-la-estafa-detectada-por-condusef/). TODO: Verify against the primary Banxico "Disposiciones aplicables a las operaciones de las Instituciones de Crédito y de la Financiera Rural en materia de pagos electrónicos" text.
- **Severity/frequency:** Only **24.3%** of the pesos claimed as fraud were returned to users in Jan–Mar 2026 (1.265bn of 5.201bn pesos claimed); in full-year 2023 the return rate was closer to **15%** (5,165M pesos returned of ~33,329M claimed). **VERIFIED** — same sources as above.
- **Why unsolved:** Existing controls (OTP, device binding) authenticate *that it's really the customer* pressing "confirm" — they do nothing when the customer is the one being deceived. The October 2025 MTU (transaction-limit) rule (see §4) caps *damage*, not *incidence*. No Mexican bank has a universal pre-settlement "cooling-off + destination-risk-score" step comparable to the UK's APP-fraud reimbursement regime; Mexico's regime instead defaults to "user authorized it, user eats the loss."

### #2 — SIM swapping enabling full account takeover
- **Population affected:** Official channel-specific numbers are small and clearly under-reported — CONDUSEF's "Soy Usuario" telecom-complaints portal logged only **17 cases in 2023 and 19 in 2024** (78% concentrated in Movistar and Bait), which is implausibly low relative to the broader identity-theft claims CONDUSEF received (**2,300–6,500 per year, 2019–2022**, a related but broader category). **VERIFIED, but likely a severe undercount because most victims report to the bank/CONDUSEF under "identity theft" or "unrecognized transfer," not as SIM-swap specifically** — [mobiletime.la](https://mobiletime.la/noticias/13/04/2026/fraudes-sim-swapping-en-mexico/) (a telecom trade outlet; treat the exact counts as lower-confidence, but the concentration-in-two-carriers finding and the general phenomenon are corroborated by CONDUSEF/BBVA explainers below).
- **Specific pain:** Attacker convinces/bribes/social-engineers a carrier into porting the victim's number to a new SIM, then intercepts SMS OTPs used for banking login and transaction confirmation, enabling **total account drain** in one session.
- **Severity/frequency evidence:** CONDUSEF issued a specific consumer alert on SIM swapping in Feb 2024, describing it as an active, growing vector. **VERIFIED** — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2024/02/21/que-es-el-sim-swapping-y-por-que-la-condusef-pidio-tener-cuidado-con-ello/), [BBVA México explainer](https://www.bbva.mx/personas/centro-de-ayuda/que-es-sim-swapping.html). A cited April 2026 item states Mexico's Supreme Court (SCJN) fixed legal responsibility on mobile operators for SIM swapping — **UNVERIFIED against a primary SCJN source**, flagging for follow-up since it would materially change the liability picture.
- **Why unsolved:** SMS-OTP remains a widely used second factor in Mexican banking; carriers' identity-verification for SIM replacement is the weak link and sits outside the bank's control entirely, so a bank-side transaction/behavioral stream is the *only* place left to catch it (e.g., abrupt device/session change followed immediately by a high-value transfer to a new payee).
- **Mexico-specific?** The mechanism (SIM swap) is global, but the concentration in specific low-KYC carriers (Movistar/Bait prepaid lines) and the weak telecom-side verification process is a Mexico-specific aggravating factor.

### #3 — Montadeudas (predatory "gota a gota" loan apps with extortion)
- **Population affected:** Mexico City's Policía Cibernética alone logged **5,800+ reports** in Jan–Feb 2026; separately, Excélsior reported **1,073 illegal/unregistered lending apps** operating in Mexico. **VERIFIED** — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2026/07/07/montadeudas-15-pasos-clave-para-evitar-fraudes-con-apps-de-prestamos-segun-la-pdi/), [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/nacional/cuidado-con-los-montadeudas/1744791).
- **Specific pain:** Apps present as legitimate lenders, are **not registered with CNBV or SIPRES**, extract full phone permissions (contacts, camera, photos, location, call log) at install, then — once the (often usurious, undisclosed) repayment is missed — **extort the victim and their entire contact list** with threats, manipulated/doctored images, and fake violent content. Named repeatedly in reporting: Starpresta, OkCredito, LemonCash, ProPrestamos, CrediYa, Gira Cash, Rapikash, MonedaYa, PrestaRapido, OkPrestamo. **VERIFIED** — same sources.
- **Why unsolved:** These operate entirely outside the regulated financial system (no CNBV authorization), so conventional bank/CNBV supervisory tools don't reach them; CONDUSEF's April-2023 "Fraude Financiero" microsite is a *lookup/verification* tool, not a blocking control, and enforcement is reactive (PDI/Policía Cibernética after the fact). **VERIFIED (existence and limits of the microsite)** — search synthesis citing CONDUSEF launch.
- **Mexico-specific?** Yes — this predatory-lending-plus-extortion pattern via WhatsApp/social-media-advertised apps preying on financially excluded/underbanked users is a documented Mexico/LatAm phenomenon, distinct from generic phishing.

### #4 — Phishing/smishing/vishing as the mass entry vector
- **Population affected:** The Competitive Intelligence Unit (CIU) reported **13.5 million Mexicans were phishing victims in 2024**; a separate N+ headline cites **"20 million victims in 2024"** — **these two figures conflict and could not be reconciled from available sources; flag both, treat exact headcount as UNVERIFIED, treat "double-digit millions of Mexicans annually" as the safer claim.**
- **Specific pain:** Of victims, CIU reports **23.1% lost money, averaging 8,750 pesos** per loss (2024/2025 CIU study). A separate figure states the average cost of online fraud is ~3,525 pesos — **these two average-loss figures also disagree** (different studies/populations); report both, do not average them.
- **Severity/frequency evidence:** CONDUSEF: **76.9%** of reported frauds concentrate in three vishing/smishing patterns — fraudulent calls impersonating the bank, SMS/instant-message requests for information under pretext of "suspicious charges," and fake websites cloning financial institutions. **VERIFIED** — [CIU](https://www.theciu.com/publicaciones-2/2025/6/16/phishing-en-mxico-amenaza-creciente-y-llamado-a-la-accin), [Expansión](https://expansion.mx/tecnologia/2025/06/18/phishing-crece-en-mexico-asi-roban-tus-datos).
- **Why unsolved:** This is the upstream credential/trust-compromise step; it happens off the bank's transaction rail (phone call, SMS, fake site) so a transaction-monitoring system can only catch its *downstream effects* (the resulting transfer/charge), not the phishing act itself.

## 3. Fraud typology catalogue (Mexico) — detectable from a transaction/behavioral stream?

| Typology | Mechanism | Detectable from transaction stream? |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing / smishing (credential theft) | Fake SMS/email/site harvests login+OTP | **Precursor is NOT detectable** (happens off-rail); the **resulting fraudulent login/transfer is** detectable via device/session change, new-payee, velocity |
| Vishing / "falso ejecutivo bancario" | Phone call impersonating bank employee, walks victim through "verification" that is actually authorizing a transfer/loan advance | Precursor not detectable; the **resulting SPEI transfer to a new, unfamiliar payee, often just under the MTU limit, under time pressure** is a strong detectable signal |
| SIM swapping | Carrier port-out hijacks SMS OTP | Precursor is telecom-side, invisible to the bank; **detectable proxy: sudden device/SIM change + new-device login + immediate high-value transfer** in the same session |
| Fraudulent/authorized SPEI transfers (APP fraud) | Victim personally authorizes transfer to scammer-controlled CLABE | **Partially detectable pre-settlement**: new beneficiary, deviation from historical transfer pattern, round-number/full-balance transfers, odd hour, beneficiary CLABE matched against known-fraud databases (e.g., CONDUSEF's Monitor de Reportes). **Not detectable post-settlement** — Banxico rules make settled SPEI transfers irrevocable |
| Card-not-present / e-commerce fraud | Stolen card data used online | **Classically detectable**: geolocation mismatch, merchant-category anomalies, velocity, device fingerprint |
| Montadeudas / predatory loan apps | Unregistered app disburses/collects microloans, then extorts using harvested phone data | Loan disbursement/collection **transactions are partially detectable** (small deposits from unregistered/unlicensed-looking counterparties, aggressive repeat debit attempts) but **the extortion itself (data misuse, threats) is entirely outside the transaction stream** |
| Core-infrastructure attack (2018 SPEI-style) | Compromise of a bank's own SPEI-connection application layer to inject phantom transfer orders | **Not detectable from a consumer transaction/behavioral stream** — this is bank-side infrastructure/operational-risk security, covered by CUB and Banxico's participant-security rules, not a fintech overlay product |
| CoDi/DiMo-specific fraud | QR-code spoofing, phone-number-linked account changes | Some signals detectable (mismatched linked-number changes, unusual CoDi charge requests) but low relevance given CoDi's near-abandonment (18% YoY transaction decline, 0.1% population usage in 2024) |

## 4. Liability & regulatory reality

- **Core liability rule (as currently explained by CONDUSEF for a documented SPEI phishing scam):** SPEI payments are identified **only** by CLABE / account number / card number — the beneficiary's displayed name is not a verifying identifier under Banxico's electronic-payments rules. Once a transfer settles into the destination account, it is **firm, irrevocable, and enforceable against third parties**. Practical effect: if the customer personally authorized the transfer (even under deception), **neither the sending nor receiving bank is automatically liable**, and the loss falls on the user. **VERIFIED as CONDUSEF's own public explanation, but I could not independently pull the primary Banxico circular text to quote the exact article/section — mark the precise legal citation as TODO: Verify.** — [Infobae, Sep 2025](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2025/09/20/fraude-con-transferencias-electronicas-asi-opera-la-estafa-detectada-por-condusef/)
- **CNBV — Circular Única de Bancos (CUB):** Prudential framework for credit institutions covering capital, comprehensive risk management (credit/market/**operational**), accounting, and regulatory reporting; assigns the institution's Director General responsibility for the information-security internal-control system (confidentiality, integrity, availability). **VERIFIED (existence and content summary)** — [CNBV normativity PDF](https://www.cnbv.gob.mx/Normatividad/Disposiciones%20de%20car%C3%A1cter%20general%20aplicables%20a%20las%20instituciones%20de%20cr%C3%A9dito.pdf), [Vermont Solutions explainer](https://vermont-solutions.com/es-mx/glosario/cnbv-cub/)
- **Ley Fintech (Ley para Regular las Instituciones de Tecnología Financiera):** ~80 cybersecurity-related requirements for authorized IFC/IFPE entities; requires operational/accounting/security infrastructure and controls; 2026 CNBV update reportedly adds **mandatory 72-hour incident reporting** and raises minimum capital to **500,000 UDIs for IFPEs**. **VERIFIED (summary-level)** — [Hackmetrix](https://blog.hackmetrix.com/requisitos-ley-fintech/), [ViaUno](https://viauno.mx/cnbv-nueva-regulacion-fintechs-2026/) — TODO: Verify exact article numbers against the primary Ley Fintech / CNBV resolution text.
- **MTU — Monto Transaccional del Usuario (the single most concrete, dated, Mexico-specific 2025–2026 rule change found):** Per the Circular Única de Bancos (as amended, 2024), all banks must let users set a personal transaction cap for third-party electronic operations (transfers, SPEI, CoDi, DiMo) from digital channels. **Oct 1, 2025**: banks were required to have the feature live; unconfigured accounts default to **1,500 UDIs (~12,800 pesos)**. **Jan 1, 2026**: user configuration becomes mandatory; if still undefined, the bank sets a limit based on the account's transaction history. Explicit stated purpose: **cap the damage** from account takeover/fraud, not prevent the fraud attempt itself. **VERIFIED** — [Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/mexico/actualidad-mx/banxico-confirma-multa-de-12800-pesos-por-no-activar-el-mtu-en-transferencias-como-hacerlo-paso-a-paso/), [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2025/09/26/asi-queda-el-limite-de-transferencias-bancarias-a-partir-del-1-de-octubre/), [Expansión](https://expansion.mx/finanzas-personales/2025/10/01/llego-fecha-limite-definir-mtu-cuenta-bancaria), [CONCANACO](https://www.concanaco.org/blog/normativas-y-regulaciones-20/monto-transaccional-del-usuario-mtu-1018)
- **Biometrics rule:** CNBV reportedly requires facial-recognition identification for N3/N4 account holders on cash deposits/withdrawals above **140,000 pesos**; separately, a mid-2026 push toward mandatory biometric data collection was noted to create friction specifically for older adults. **UNVERIFIED against a primary CNBV disposición — sourced only from secondary news aggregation** ([Expansión](https://expansion.mx/economia/2026/07/06/bancos-mexico-datos-biometricos-cuidar-adultos-mayores)).
- **June 2026 Banxico rule (separate from MTU):** Standardizes the mobile-transfer UX across banks and creates "Nivel 2 Bis" accounts for micro/small merchants (up to ~132,000 pesos/month, most from digital payments); compliance deadline **Dec 14, 2026**. This is about UX standardization/financial inclusion, **not explicitly a fraud-liability change** — despite headlines invoking "fraud." **VERIFIED (content), fraud linkage UNVERIFIED** — [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/06/19/haces-transferencias-desde-tu-celular-banxico-cambia-las-reglas-para-spei-codi-y-dimo/).
- **No evidence found of a universal Mexican "new-payee cooling-off period"** analogous to some other markets (the closest analog found is that first transfers to a brand-new destination account "may go through anti-fraud validations that add minutes or hours" at individual banks' discretion — not a codified nationwide rule). **UNVERIFIED / not confirmed as a formal regulation** — treat as bank-specific practice, not law.

## 5. Existing defenses and their gaps

| Defense | Who | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| MTU (self-set transaction cap, default 1,500 UDIs) | All banks, mandatory since Oct 2025 | Only limits maximum *loss per period*; does not detect or stop a scam in progress; a user can be coached by a scammer to raise their own MTU |
| Real-time transfer risk-scoring ("Fraud Alert") | Nu México | Analyzes destination-account risk signals and shows a warning before confirmation — one of the few pre-settlement interventions found. **VERIFIED** — [Nu blog](https://international.nubank.com.br/es/clientes/nu-lanza-alerta-de-estafa-para-proteger-en-tiempo-real-el-dinero-de-sus-clientes/). Gap: proprietary/single-bank; does not help once money reaches a receiving bank without similar controls |
| Facial biometrics for large cash movements (N3/N4, >140,000 pesos) | CNBV/multiple banks (claimed) | Only applies above a high threshold; does not address the modal fraud (median SPEI transaction is well under 10,000 pesos, i.e. 94% of operations) |
| CONDUSEF Portal/Monitor de Fraudes Financieros | CONDUSEF, since 2020, expanded April 2023 | User-driven lookup tool, not integrated into banks' transfer-confirmation flow; requires the victim to think to check it *before* transferring |
| SMS/OTP two-factor authentication | All banks | Defeated wholesale by SIM swapping; also the vector abused in phishing (victim relays OTP to scammer) |
| Bank complaint/BEF public shaming | CONDUSEF | After-the-fact; does not prevent fraud, only creates reputational pressure and drives the 24.3%/15% partial refund rates seen above |

## 7. Product opportunity hypotheses

**1. Pre-settlement APP-fraud circuit breaker for SPEI transfers.**
Given that Mexican rule treats a *settled* SPEI transfer as irrevocable and puts the loss on the authorizing user (§4), the only real lever is intervening *before* settlement. A real-time transaction/behavioral stream would enable: flagging first-time beneficiaries, transfers that deviate sharply from the user's historical amount/frequency/time-of-day pattern, transfers sized just under the user's MTU threshold (a likely scammer-coaching pattern), and cross-referencing the destination CLABE against a fraud-report database (à la CONDUSEF's Monitor de Reportes or Nu's internal model) — then inserting a friction step (delay, re-confirmation, human-language warning) exactly where Nu's "Fraud Alert" already operates for one bank. This is the highest-leverage opportunity because it attacks pain point #1 (the largest, ~1.5M complaints/quarter) at its one interceptable moment.

**2. Account-takeover / SIM-swap early-warning layer.**
A behavioral stream combining device/session fingerprint changes with transaction requests would enable detecting the classic takeover signature: sudden new-device login, immediately followed by a high-value transfer or full-balance withdrawal attempt to a new payee. Since SIM swapping itself happens outside the bank's visibility (carrier-side), this is the only bank-observable proxy signal — directly useful given SIM swapping's likely under-reporting (pain point #2).

**3. Montadeudas early-detection for consumers.**
A transaction stream could flag disbursement/collection patterns typical of unregistered lenders (small, round-figure deposits from entities not in CNBV/SIPRES registries; unusually aggressive, high-frequency debit attempts shortly after) and warn the user before the extortion phase begins. This only reaches the *financial* trace of montadeudas (loan cash flows) — it cannot detect or stop the extortion itself (data misuse, threats), which happens entirely outside any transaction rail; would need to be scoped as a narrow early-warning signal, not a full solution.

**4. Vishing/"falso ejecutivo" real-time correlation.**
If a bank had (or could partner for) call-metadata or a user-reportable "I just got a suspicious call" signal, combining it with the transaction stream would let the system hold/step-up-verify any transfer initiated within minutes of contact from a number pattern matching CONDUSEF's reported scam-number lists. This is more speculative since it requires either telecom data-sharing or user self-report at the moment of the call, which is friction-heavy — weaker than hypotheses 1–2 on feasibility grounds, but addresses a documented dominant typology (76.9% of frauds concentrate in call/SMS/fake-site vishing-style patterns).
