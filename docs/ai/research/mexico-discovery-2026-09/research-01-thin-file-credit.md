# Research 01 — Consumer credit access, thin-file / no-credit-history users, and credit building in Mexico

Scope: Mexico only. All figures are dated; VERIFIED FACT = has a working, checkable URL from a primary or clearly-sourced outlet; UNVERIFIED = could not confirm origin, or is anecdotal/single-source.

---

## Top pain points (ranked)

### 1. Roughly two-thirds of Mexican adults have no formal credit product at all — "thin file" is the majority condition, not the exception
- **Population affected:** 62.7% of adults aged 18–70 (2024) had **no** formal credit product of any kind (bank credit card, department-store card, payroll/personal loan, mortgage, auto/group credit). Only **37.3%** had at least one formal credit product. VERIFIED — INEGI/CNBV, *Encuesta Nacional de Inclusión Financiera (ENIF) 2024*, published 13 March 2025 (fieldwork June–Aug 2024). [ENIF 2024 report](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2025/enif/ENIF2024_RR.pdf)
- Even fewer have a **bank** credit card specifically: 15.7% of adults (2024), up from 10.5% in 2021. Department-store cards remain more common (22.6%) — these usually report to bureaus but carry weaker underwriting and higher effective cost. Same source.
- Gender and regional gaps persist: 36.1% of women vs. 38.8% of men have any credit (2024); Southeast/Center-South regions lag the Northeast (46.2%) and Northwest (46.0%). Same source.
- **Why unsolved:** the gap is not just "won't lend" — 20.7% of never-borrowers cite not meeting requirements, and self-exclusion (not liking debt, 38.4%) is entangled with a rational fear of rejection and high cost. Both bureau-based underwriting and self-selection reinforce the same exclusion. **Mexico-specific in degree** (informality rate below), but the credit-history circularity itself is generic to any emerging market with a bureau-centric system.

### 2. Rejection is dominated by "no credit history" and "no verifiable income," which are structurally linked to labor informality
- **Population affected:** 17% of adults report having applied for credit and been rejected at least once (2021, unchanged since 2018/2015). Of those rejected, the leading stated reasons were: problems with the credit bureau record 36%, insufficient/unverifiable income 27%, **no credit history at all 19%**, missing documents 13%, no guarantor/aval 11%. VERIFIED — CNBV/INEGI, *ENIF 2021, Reporte de Resultados*, pp. 27–28. [PDF](https://www.cnbv.gob.mx/Inclusi%C3%B3n/Anexos%20Inclusin%20Financiera/Reporte_Resultados_ENIF_2021.pdf)
- "No credit history" as a rejection reason is most cited by the youngest cohort (18–29), consistent with them just entering the labor/credit market. Same source, p. 28.
- Underlying driver: **54.8% of the employed population works in labor informality** (INEGI ENOE, monthly series, e.g. 54.9% in Oct 2025, 54.8% Dec 2025). VERIFIED. [INEGI ENOE bulletins](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2025/enoe/enoe2025_11.pdf). Informal workers generally cannot produce payroll receipts (nómina/CFDI) or formal proof-of-income documents that banks require, which is exactly the "sin documentos"/"ingreso insuficiente" rejection bucket above.
- On the small-business side: an estimated ~90% of Mexican MSMEs (of ~5.5 million, which sustain 70.6% of employment) lack access to formal financing, and only 10.72% of MSMEs that request a bank loan obtain one, largely because 63% of small commercial establishments operate informally and run on cash, which produces no auditable transaction trail. UNVERIFIED aggregator figures (El Imparcial, citing Coparmex/BBVA México, dated 28 Jul 2026) — directionally consistent with CNBV's own ENAFIN-based infographics but the exact 90%/10.72% figures are second-hand and could not be traced to a CNBV/INEGI primary table in this pass. [El Imparcial](https://www.elimparcial.com/dinero/2026/07/28/nueve-de-cada-10-mipymes-en-mexico-se-quedan-sin-acceso-a-creditos-bancarios-por-operar-en-la-informalidad-y-depender-del-uso-de-efectivo-diario/), CNBV ENAFIN-based infographic exists but was not itself pulled: [CNBV infographic](https://www.cnbv.gob.mx/Inclusi%C3%B3n/Anexos%20Inclusin%20Financiera/Infografias/SNEF_2024_PRES_Acceso_al_financiamiento_de_micro_y_pequenas_empresas.pdf).
- **Why unsolved:** traditional bureau scoring (Buró de Crédito, Círculo de Crédito) is built almost entirely on formal-credit repayment history. If you've never had formal credit or never had formal payroll income, there is structurally nothing for the model to score — a closed loop. **Generic-to-emerging-markets mechanism**, but the *scale* (>half the workforce informal) is a Mexico/Latin-America-specific intensity, not a US/EU one.

### 3. When rejected or excluded, people fall back to informal credit that is materially more expensive and, at the extreme, predatory/criminal ("gota a gota")
- **Population affected:** in 2021, 31% of adults used some form of informal credit (family loans 22%, friends/acquaintances 13%, cajas de ahorro 6-7%, empeño/pawning 3-5%), down from higher 2018 levels post-pandemic. VERIFIED — ENIF 2021, pp. 24–25. [PDF](https://www.cnbv.gob.mx/Inclusi%C3%B3n/Anexos%20Inclusin%20Financiera/Reporte_Resultados_ENIF_2021.pdf)
- At the harsher end: unregulated "gota a gota" / "montadeudas" digital loan-shark schemes charge **daily interest of 10%–40%** on small tickets (MXN 500–2,000), which annualizes far beyond any regulated product. UNVERIFIED exact annualization and "36% of adults used informal credit in 2022 with 59% paying >30%/yr" figure — sourced from an aggregator article (El Financiero, Nov 2023) that does not name a primary survey; treat as indicative, not official. [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/mundo-empresa/2023/11/28/creditos-informales-en-mexico-una-amenaza-que-te-hara-querer-pensarlo-dos-veces/)
- This has a visible **victimization/crime dimension**, not just a pricing one: Mexico City's Consejo Ciudadano para la Seguridad y Justicia logged 4,907 "montadeudas" (loan-shark app) reports in 2023, rising 29% to 6,345 in the first seven months of 2024, and has cumulatively identified **1,046 predatory lending apps** since 2021 (57 new in 2024). Reports of extortion tied to these loans grew 157% in one comparison period. Mexico City + State of Mexico account for 86% of reports. VERIFIED (Consejo Ciudadano data reported by multiple outlets). [Buzos](https://buzos.com.mx/noticia/aumenta-29-reportes-de-montadeudas-durante-2024), [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/comunidad/denuncias-por-extorsiones-de-prestamos-bajo-el-esquema-de-gota-a-gota-crecieron-157), [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/metropoli/cdmx/consejo-ciudadano-recibe-58-reportes-por-prestamos-gota-gota-en-los-ultimos-10-dias/) — note these are CDMX-metro counts, not national.
- A widely repeated claim that "30% of the population is affected by gota a gota" and that this comes from ENIF 2024 could **not** be confirmed against the actual ENIF 2024 primary report (which does not contain a "gota a gota" line item in the results I could pull) — **flag as UNVERIFIED / likely misattribution**, pending check of the ENIF 2024 microdata/full annex tables (only the short "Reporte de Resultados" was checked here).
- **Why unsolved:** informal/predatory lenders exist precisely because they require no bureau history and can enforce repayment through social pressure or coercion instead of legal collateral — i.e., they solve the "no data" problem banks can't solve, at a cost paid in interest and personal risk. This dynamic (informal credit filling a bank-access gap) is generic to emerging markets; the specific "gota a gota"/Colombian-origin loan-shark branding and app-based scaling in CDMX is a **regionally documented, not uniquely Mexican, phenomenon** (same schemes reported in Colombia, Peru).

### 4. Even formal/regulated small-ticket credit is priced far above what banked, higher-income borrowers pay — the thin-file segment that *does* get approved still bears a steep "inclusion premium"
- **Population affected:** anyone whose ticket size or risk profile puts them in small-loan / no-collateral segments.
- Credit cards: system-wide effective rate (TEPP) for all clients was **22.8%** annual (June 2023), but for revolving/non-full-payers specifically **36.9%**, and cardholders with a credit limit ≤ MXN 5,000 paid a TEPP of **53.9%** vs. **21.3%** for limits over MXN 500,000 — i.e., the smallest, most likely first-time/thin-file limits are priced ~2.5x higher. VERIFIED — Banxico, *Indicadores Básicos de Tarjetas de Crédito*, datos a junio de 2023. [PDF](https://www.banxico.org.mx/publicaciones-y-prensa/rib-tarjetas-de-credito/%7BD0CFE913-D1B5-4776-6F99-4BE8A09A245D%7D.pdf)
- Personal loans/microcredit: system-wide weighted average rate for personal loans was **43.1%** (Sept 2023–Aug 2024 vintage), dominated by Banco Azteca (83% of originations). Individual microcredit averaged **72.7%**, dominated by Compartamos (95.3% of loans, ~72-79% rates); group microcredit ranged from **61.2%** (groups >15 people) to **88.2%** (groups ≤5 people) — smaller, presumably less-established groups pay the most. VERIFIED — Banxico, *Indicadores Básicos de Créditos Personales y Microcréditos*, datos a agosto de 2024. [PDF](https://www.banxico.org.mx/publicaciones-y-prensa/rib-creditos-personales/%7B0C250881-C26D-604B-39E6-BA83080DB516%7D.pdf)
- **Why unsolved:** this is risk-based pricing operating exactly as designed under uncertainty — lenders charge more when they have less data/collateral, which is rational per-loan but keeps the "graduation" path from informal to prime credit expensive and slow. This mechanism (small tickets priced highest) is **generic**, not Mexico-specific, though the specific rate levels are.

### 5. Complaint volumes show credit cards are the single most-disputed consumer credit product, indicating friction even for the already-included
- 2023: credit cards drew **39,345** CONDUSEF complaints (+1.7% YoY vs. 38,702 in 2022), within a total of 155,996 complaints against banks (+6.8% YoY). VERIFIED. [La Jornada, 19 Jan 2024](https://www.jornada.com.mx/noticia/2024/01/19/economia/repuntaron-quejas-contra-bancos-en-2023-condusef-9491)
- H1 2024: 15,577 credit-card complaints out of 63,634 total against multiple banking institutions; debit cards led with 18,133 (32% of total); "unrecognized charges" was the top cause (22%). BBVA México (21%) and Banamex (18%) concentrated the most complaints. VERIFIED. [Milenio](https://www.milenio.com/negocios/condusef-recibio-63-mil-reclamaciones-usuarios-bancarios-2024)
- **Why relevant but secondary:** this is a service-quality/fraud signal for people who *already* have credit, not a thin-file-specific pain point — included here for completeness but it is not primarily an inclusion problem.

## Regulatory & data-access reality (Open Finance / Open Banking under Ley Fintech)

This is the load-bearing fact for any product idea premised on "just pull continuous transaction data via Open Banking APIs in Mexico." **It is not there yet, as of 2026.**

- Mexico's *Ley para Regular las Instituciones de Tecnología Financiera* ("Ley Fintech"), enacted 9 March 2018, was Latin America's first Open Banking law. Article 76 requires financial entities to build standardized APIs across **three data tiers**: (1) Open/public data, (2) Aggregated data, (3) Transactional data (requires explicit client consent). VERIFIED. [Fiskil](https://www.fiskil.com/es/open-finance/mexico), [LegalParadox](https://www.legalparadox.com/es/ley-fintech)
- **Only Tier 1 (Open Data)** has functioning secondary rules: CNBV published the *Disposiciones de carácter general* for open data (branch/ATM locations, product info) on **4 June 2020**. VERIFIED, corroborated across three independent sources. [Fiskil](https://www.fiskil.com/es/open-finance/mexico), [OpenBankingTracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/blog/open-banking-in-mexico-eight-years-of-promise-still-waiting-on-the-regulations), [LatamFintech](https://www.latamfintech.co/articles/cnbv-emite-las-primeras-reglas-y-estandares-de-open-finance-en-mexico)
- **Tiers 2 and 3 (Aggregated Data and Transactional Data) — the tiers that would actually let a third party pull a consumer's bank transaction history via a regulated, standardized API — have never had secondary rules published**, years past the legal deadline. As of the most recent sources found (dated as late as **February 2026**), CNBV has run pilots but has not issued the operative disposiciones; CNBV also faces real-terms budget cuts for 2026 amid broader austerity, which several sources flag as a headwind to finishing this work. VERIFIED (multiple independent corroborating sources, most recent dated 2026). [LatamFintech](https://www.latamfintech.co/articles/cnbv-emite-las-primeras-reglas-y-estandares-de-open-finance-en-mexico), [OpenBankingTracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/blog/open-banking-in-mexico-eight-years-of-promise-still-waiting-on-the-regulations)
- **Practical consequence:** growth in "open finance"-branded products in Mexico today happens through **bilateral, negotiated integrations** between fintechs and individual banks (screen-scraping-derived or privately negotiated feeds), not a regulator-mandated standardized API. Every aggregator has to build and maintain its own bank-by-bank connections. VERIFIED characterization, consistent across sources above.
- **Aggregators that exist in practice today** (not under the Ley Fintech Tier 2/3 regime, but via private integrations/credential-based aggregation):
  - **Belvo** — claims aggregation coverage of 60+ institutions across Mexico/Brazil/Colombia and >90% of personal/business bank accounts, plus tax-authority (SAT) data and payments initiation. VERIFIED as a company claim, not independently audited. [Belvo](https://belvo.com/solutions/aggregation/)
  - **Finerio Connect** — Mexico City-based open-finance data platform, raised ~USD 6.5M. [Finerio Connect](https://finerioconnect.com/en)
  - **Prometeo** — single API for banking data/payments across 11 LatAm countries + US. [Prometeo](https://www.legalparadox.com/categories/open-finance) (general category page; verify company site directly for Mexico-specific coverage)
  - **Palenca** — payroll/income API (not bank-transaction aggregation) specifically for gig-economy workers (Uber, Rappi, DiDi, 99, inDriver, Cornershop) — pulls earnings history and worker profile/behavioral data (acceptance rate, rating) directly from platform accounts with consent; used by 70+ financial institutions/fintechs (Banco Falabella, Rappi, Neon, Kavak cited). This is directly relevant to a "gig-worker credit scoring" angle since it substitutes for payroll/nómina proof that informal/gig workers structurally lack. VERIFIED as company claim. [Palenca](https://blog.palenca.com/como-verificar-ingresos-de-trabajadores-gig-para-originacion-de-credito-en-mexico/)
- **Credit bureau regulatory frame** (separate from Open Banking): Mexico's two Sociedades de Información Crediticia (SICs) — **Buró de Crédito** and **Círculo de Crédito** — operate under the *Ley para Regular las Sociedades de Información Crediticia*. They only have data on formal-credit repayment behavior; per one industry estimate, bureaus hold usable records for only ~49% of the population's borrowing behavior. UNVERIFIED precise 49% figure (appeared in a vendor blog post without a traceable primary citation) — directionally consistent with the ENIF 2024 finding that 62.7% have no formal credit product, but treat the exact number as approximate.

**Bottom line for product design:** a hackathon concept that assumes "we'll just call the Open Banking transactional API" is not buildable on the actual regulated rails in Mexico today. Anything using bank-transaction-level cash flow data must go through a private aggregator (Belvo/Finerio/Prometeo) under bilateral bank consent flows, or through a payroll/gig-platform API (Palenca) — both of which are commercial products, not free regulatory infrastructure, and both have partial, not universal, bank coverage.

## Product opportunity hypotheses (what continuous transaction/cash-flow data would enable)

These are hypotheses for the hackathon team to validate further, not conclusions — flagged where they rely on assumptions.

1. **Cash-flow-based "graduation" underwriting for informal/gig workers using Palenca-style income APIs + bank aggregation (Belvo/Finerio) instead of bureau history.**
   What continuous data enables: instead of a point-in-time bureau pull (which is empty for 62.7% of adults), an underwriter could see recurring income deposits (gig-platform payouts, informal-business cash-in patterns via account aggregation, utility/rent payment regularity) over months, and use *stability and trend* rather than *formal history* as the risk signal. This directly targets Pain Points #1–#2 (no history, no verifiable income). **Caveat:** this only works for the subset of informal workers who are banked and either gig-platform-active or willing to link a bank account — it does not reach fully cash-based, unbanked workers, and it depends on private aggregator coverage since the regulated Tier-2/3 Open Banking APIs don't exist yet.

2. **A "graduation ladder" product that starts users on a small, transaction-data-informed limit (like Stori's low-limit starter card) but re-prices/expands the limit automatically as continuous account data shows sustained positive cash flow**, rather than waiting for 6–12 months of bureau-visible repayment. What continuous data enables: faster, evidence-based limit increases and rate reductions instead of the current one-size-fits-most microcredit pricing (61–88% for group microcredit regardless of individual behavior beyond group size). Targets Pain Point #4 (the "inclusion premium"). **Caveat:** needs a live account-data feed (not just onboarding-time snapshot) to actually beat the current group-based/tiered pricing that incumbents already use as a crude proxy for the same thing.

3. **An early-warning / anti-predatory-lending signal built on transaction data:** if a user's transaction pattern shows daily/weekly outflows consistent with "gota a gota"/montadeudas repayment structures (small, high-frequency, round-number debits to unfamiliar counterparties), a fintech could flag the user as at-risk and proactively offer a formal refinancing/consolidation product. Targets Pain Point #3. **Caveat:** this is a novel-pattern-detection hypothesis, not something any cited player is confirmed to be doing today — validate feasibility (do these transactions even appear as identifiable bank debits, or are gota-a-gota payments typically cash/app-wallet based, which would be invisible to bank-transaction data?) before building.

4. **MSME/informal-merchant underwriting using digital payment-acceptance data (card terminal, QR, e-wallet inflows) as a substitute for the accounting records informal businesses don't keep.** What continuous data enables: daily/weekly revenue inflow visibility that substitutes for the "no legal constitution / cash-only" barrier cited as the #1 reason ~90% of MSMEs lack bank credit. This is a **generic embedded-finance play already partly addressed by BNPL/point-of-sale lenders (Kueski, Konfío-style players)** — the differentiated angle for a hackathon would need to be a specific underserved merchant segment or a specific data source competitors aren't yet using. **Caveat:** the 90%/10.72% MSME figures underlying this are UNVERIFIED (see above) and should be re-confirmed against CNBV/ENAFIN primary tables before being used as the headline justification.
