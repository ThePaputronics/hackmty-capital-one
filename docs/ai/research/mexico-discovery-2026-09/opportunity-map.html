Recommended niche

Pre-settlement scam interception for SPEI transfers

Mexican payment rules identify a transfer only by CLABE — the beneficiary name shown on screen verifies nothing — and a settled SPEI transfer is firm, irrevocable and enforceable against third parties. When someone is talked into authorizing the transfer themselves, neither bank is liable and the victim absorbs the loss. CONDUSEF logged 1.515 million fraud complaints in Q1 2026 alone and only 24.3% of the pesos claimed came back.

Because a settled transfer can never be recalled, the only place left to save someone's money is the few seconds before they press confirm — so that is where the intelligence belongs.

Three constraints that bound every option

Each of these was checked against primary sources before ranking. Two of them disqualify otherwise-attractive ideas outright.

Verified · 3 sources

Open Finance transactional APIs do not exist

CNBV published Ley Fintech Art. 76 rules for the open data tier only (DOF, 4 Jun 2020). The aggregated and transactional tiers were still unpublished as of Feb 2026. Never pitch “Mexico's Open Finance API” — say “via a Belvo-style aggregator.”

Licensing wall

The prototype cannot move money

Holding or moving client funds needs CNBV IFPE authorization, a 12–18 month process. Everything we build stays in the forecast, flag and nudge lane — which also caps what “agentic action” can honestly mean on stage.

Data blindness

Cash hides the largest segment

Labor informality is 54.8% — 32.6M workers, Q1 2026 — and cash was still ~88% of transactions in 2023. A product premised on reading a transaction stream structurally cannot see this population.

Ranked opportunities

Ordered by magnitude, evidence strength, differentiation, and whether real-time data genuinely drives the output. Entries 4 through 8 are recorded as assessed and rejected, not as alternatives.

01

SPEI scam interception

Recommended Track 3 + 1 Households & salaried workers

Score the transfer in the seconds before confirmation and insert proportionate friction with a plain-language explanation naming the scam pattern. The one interceptable moment in a rail that is irreversible by design.

1.515Mfraud complaints, Q1 2026 — up 31.5% YoY
24.3%of claimed pesos returned to users
~75%of all complaints against banks are fraud
$12,800default MTU cap, in force since Oct 2025

The Mexico-specific signal nobody has a mature model for: a transfer sized just under the user's own MTU is a plausible scammer-coaching tell — and it only exists because of the October 2025 rule. Incumbents have had months, not years, of data on it.

Why existing defenses miss it

MTU caps damage, not incidence — and a scammer can coach the victim into raising their own limit. OTP and device binding confirm the real customer pressed confirm, which is exactly what happens in this fraud. Nu's Fraud Alert does score destinations pre-settlement, but it is one bank and proprietary. CONDUSEF's fraud portal is a lookup the victim must think to consult first.

02

The day-17 tax trap for PYMEs

Backup A Track 2 Formal PYMEs, Título II

Provisional ISR is computed on ingresos nominales — revenue invoiced, not collected — and falls due on the 17th of each month. A supplier owes tax on money it has not received. RESICO's cash-basis carve-out exists precisely because this trap is recognized.

66 daysaverage time to collect an invoice
20%of invoiced value collected only after 90+ days
8%of firms had financing access (2017)
7 in 10would refuse bank credit — 57.9% say too expensive

Highest differentiation of any option. NAFIN's reverse factoring only reaches suppliers of enrolled large buyers; Xepelin, Konfío and Covalto price the receivable and need a confirming buyer. No reviewed product targets “tax date collides with unpaid receivables” as a distinct problem.

Known weakness

CFDI is not a real-time feed. SAT's Descarga Masiva is credential-gated, capped at 2,000 XML/day, two full-content requests per date range, 48-hour processing. The “constant data flow” half of the theme would have to come from bank or POS data instead — and a monthly cycle does not animate like a live interception.

03

Cash-flow graduation and repricing

Backup B Track 1 Thin-file consumers

Continuous re-scoring that lowers price as behavior proves out, instead of a one-time approval. Thin-file is the majority condition in Mexico, and the people who qualify only for small tickets pay dramatically more for them.

62.7%of adults hold no formal credit product at all
53.9%card rate at limits ≤$5,000 — vs 21.3% above $500,000
72.7%average individual microcredit rate
88.2%group microcredit, upper range
Why it ranks third despite the strongest numbers

Commercially crowded — Stori, Klar and Kueski already underwrite on alternative data, and Compartamos and Banco Azteca already reach this segment at scale. They solved access, not cost. The research also flags the causal chain as generic to emerging markets: Mexico's distinguishing feature is the scale of informality, not a unique structural quirk. Credit scoring also carries the heaviest fairness exposure of any option here.

04

Montadeudas early warning

Component, not product

Vivid and genuinely Mexico-specific — 1,073 illegal lending apps, 5,800+ CDMX reports in Jan–Feb 2026 — but the actual harm is contact-list extortion and doctored images, which happen entirely off the transaction rail. Only the disbursements and repeat debits are visible. Best used as a detector inside a larger product.

05

Quincena runway forecasting

Cliché-adjacent

The 15th/30th pay rhythm is a real institutional fact, and only 43% of adults could face an emergency using one month of savings. But this sits directly on top of the single most repeated hackathon pattern — the budgeting dashboard. Viable as a component; fatal as a headline.

06

Total BNPL exposure view

Evidence too thin

A real information gap — BNPL debt splits across Kueski, Aplazo, Nelo and Mercado Pago with no consolidated view. But the claim that all providers report to the bureaus is unverified sector-wide, and every market-size figure found failed verification.

07

Micro-merchant books from payments

Already commercial

66% of ~8.1M microenterprises keep no accounting records at all. Real problem — but Clip Capital and Mercado Crédito already monetize exactly this signal, at 69–132% CAT.

08

Retail investor tooling

Poor track fit

Ranked last on the research's own honest verdict. AFORE choice inertia and GAT nominal-versus-real disclosure are point-in-time enrollment and disclosure problems, not patterns that unfold in a transaction stream. The two stream-native sub-angles are just narrower versions of entry 01.

The minimum viable prototype

One pipeline, one surface, one measurable claim.

Stage 1

Synthetic SPEI-shaped event stream — transfers, beneficiaries, sessions, devices

Stage 2

Streaming enrichment and per-user behavioral baseline

Stage 3

Detection agent scores the pending transfer

Guardrail

Risk agent can downgrade or block the proposed action

Stage 4

Explanation agent names the pattern in plain language

Signals the stream feeds

  • First-time beneficiary CLABE
  • Deviation from the user's amount, hour and frequency baseline
  • Amount sitting just under the configured MTU
  • Session or device change immediately preceding the transfer
  • Destination-CLABE reputation across the population

Actions the agent may propose

  • Hold and step-up verification
  • A warning naming the specific scam pattern
  • A temporary MTU reduction
  • A pre-drafted CONDUSEF complaint if the user proceeds anyway

Every action human-overridable. Nothing auto-freezes real funds.

Data we can actually get

A self-authored generator for the Mexican-shaped stream; IEEE-CIS and PaySim for labeled anomaly signal, disclosed on stage as US/EU-shaped; Nessie for transaction shape; a Belvo sandbox only if we want a credible “connects to something real” beat.

The success metric

Pesos intercepted before settlement versus the same stream with detection off — with p95 detection latency and the false-positive friction rate shown beside it, so the number cannot be read as free.

Decisions needed before implementation

01

Confirm or reject the inversion. The repository's brief records a coach-led hybrid with anomaly detection inside it. The evidence supports leading with interception and using the coach as the explanation surface. That is the owner's call, and it changes challenge-brief.md.

02

Confirm the sponsor API key's scope and quota and the model provider — the brief names Gemini but marks it unverified.

03

Decide whether to wire a real aggregator sandbox (Belvo or Prometeo) for demo credibility, or run entirely on our own generator.

04

Pick the stream substrate — Kafka, Kinesis, Redis Streams or an in-process queue — against the cloud-readiness checklist.

05

Split the team across generator, stream processor, agent layer and interface.
